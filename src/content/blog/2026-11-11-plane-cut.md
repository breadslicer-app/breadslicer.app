Cutting a miniature in two is a daily job: the model does not fit, the base prints better on its own, a part needs a different pose. The cut has to give two closed solids that slice, with exactly the material of the original, and it has to be fast enough to move the plane and watch the result follow.

## Two candidates

Before writing any code we measured two ways of doing it on real miniatures:

- **A general boolean library.** [manifold](https://github.com/elalish/manifold), through its pure-Rust port [`manifold-rust`](https://crates.io/crates/manifold-rust) (Apache-2.0), cut all 40 test cases correctly, and 200 of 200 random planes on the hardest model. But each cut took 0.07 to 2.2 seconds, plus the time to import the mesh.
- **A cut of our own.** A plane cut is a much narrower problem than a general boolean: split the triangles the plane crosses, then close each opening with a flat cap. Our prototype also cut all 40 cases, in 7 to 92 ms, and 1,598 of 1,600 random planes. The two failures were on a part that passes through itself.

Neither one cuts an open mesh: a model has to be closed (or repaired first) for "inside" to mean anything.

We took both: **our own cut first, checked, with the boolean library as a fallback.**

## How the cut works

When the Cut tool opens, the model is checked once to be closed and split into its bodies. Then, for each position of the plane:

1. **The section.** The plane's intersection with the model is a set of closed loops. Loops are grouped into parts (an outline and the holes inside it), each with its own area. This takes 1 to 5 ms, so the section follows the plane live.
2. **The cut.** Only the triangles the plane crosses are split. Each piece is capped with a triangulation of its own section (we use the triangulator from `manifold-rust`), so caps of different bodies never mix.
3. **The check.** Only the edges along the cut are checked; the rest of the mesh did not change. If anything is wrong, the whole cut is redone with the boolean library.

The plane can also be **limited** to an outline you shape by its four corners. Then the cut only separates the parts of the section inside it, and you can click parts on or off. Bodies the plane does not touch go with the piece they overlap most.

Applying the cut is one undo step. Supports on the model are removed in the same step, since they no longer fit either piece. A mesh that is not closed is repaired on a copy first.

## Numbers

On 8 miniatures, 1,619 random cuts:

- **All 1,619 closed, with exactly the volume of the original.**
- **10 went through the fallback**, all on the same model, at 0.5 to 0.7 s each.
- Median cut: **38 ms**. Section: 1–5 ms. Preparing a model when the tool opens: 14–249 ms.

In the app, on a 40 mm miniature that had to be repaired first: preparing 2.5 s (mostly the repair), moving the plane 10 ms, the trial cut shown while you wait 98 ms, applying 14 ms. The trial cut runs in the background once the plane has been still for 180 ms, and the card says how many pieces you are about to get.

## The handles

The first version moved the plane from the side panel, and it was fast but awkward. The plane now has its own gizmo in the scene, drawn in the same style as the move gizmo: an arrow to move it along its normal, two rings to tilt it, a square at each corner of the limited outline that moves only within the plane, and dragging the plane itself slides the outline. The model's own gizmo hides while the cut is open, so there is only ever one set of handles on screen.

## What is next

Pins and magnet slots on the cut faces, cuts drawn as a stroke or a lasso on the model, and suggesting where to cut by the folds of the shape. Each of those builds on this cut.

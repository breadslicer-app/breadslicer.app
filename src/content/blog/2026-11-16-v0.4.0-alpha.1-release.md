Breadslicer 0.4.0-alpha.1 is out. It is the first build anyone outside our own desk can download: a native resin (MSLA) slicer for Windows, written in Rust, free software under the AGPL-3.0. It is an **alpha**. It works, we print with it, and it will still surprise you somewhere. Read "Before you print" below before sending anything to a printer.

Download it from the [release page on GitHub](https://github.com/breadslicer-app/breadslicer/releases/tag/v0.4.0-alpha.1). It is a 43 MB zip: unzip it anywhere and run `breadslicer.exe`, nothing to install.

Why "0.4"? Breadslicer started from [SoulCrafted Slicer](https://slicer.soulcrafted.it), which had already shipped 0.3.x. We kept counting from there instead of pretending to start at zero.

## What a slicer has to do, and what this one does

A resin slicer takes a model, helps you get it ready to print (place it, hollow it, support it), cuts it into layers and writes the file your printer reads. Each of those steps is in this build. Here is the tour, step by step, in the order of the four tabs: **Prepare · Hollow · Supports · Slice**.

### Prepare

- **Move, rotate and scale** with a gizmo or with Blender-style keys: G, R, S, then X, Y or Z to lock an axis. Ctrl snaps (1 mm, 15°, 10 %), Shift moves finely. Typed turns land on the tenth of a degree they show, so −37.0° really is −37.0°, not −36.99°.
- **Several plates in one scene.** Each plate is one print. Add, remove, rename them in place (double-click the name), drag models between them, slice and export one plate or all of them. One undo history and one project file for all.
- **Arrange all** packs every plate using the real shape of each model at every height, not just its shadow from above: a wing may pass over a small model if they never come within 1 mm. Models that don't fit go to a new plate. Models you placed by hand can stay where you put them.
- **Fill the plate** with copies of what you selected, mixed evenly if you selected several.
- **Auto-orient** with a Quick (about half a second on a 1.9 M-triangle miniature) and a Full search, choosing between fewer supports, less peel force or a shorter print. Paint areas with *Protect detail* and the search avoids putting supports there.
- **Cut** a model with a plane, across the whole model or only inside an outline you shape by its corners, with handles in the scene. Click the parts of the section you want cut.
- **Repair** broken meshes (cracks, holes, double faces, parts touching on an edge, pieces turned inside out) so they slice. See [Mesh repair that makes broken models printable](/reports/mesh-repair/).
- **Overhang map**: the model is painted where it needs support, live, and the paint fades where a support tip already holds it.

### Hollow

- **The preview is the result.** What you see while you drag the wall slider is, to the thousandth of a millimetre, the cavity you get. See [Exact hollowing](/reports/exact-hollowing/).
- **Wall thickness and minimum cavity width**: thin parts stay solid instead of getting a cavity nobody could wash out.
- **Drain holes**: straight or tapered, with automatic or fixed depth, and an optional plug printed next to the model. A drain that would not reach the cavity is not drilled, and the tool tells you.
- **Solid areas** drawn with a lasso, from any camera angle.

### Supports

- **Three presets by model size**: Miniatures (20–65 mm), Figures (65–150 mm), Statues (150 mm and up). Each one is editable on a live drawing of the support, in 3D or as a section to scale, where every dimension is a field you can type or drag.
- **Hand tools**: Add (keys 1–4 pick the tip size), Move tip, Brace and Remove (click, box or brush). Hand edits are saved in the project, and *Recalculate* keeps them when a set has to be redrawn.
- **Supports follow their model**: move it on the plate or turn it about the vertical and the supports go with it; tilt, raise or scale it and they are generated again in the same undo step.
- **One raft for all**: copies on a plate stand on one shared line raft, packed tighter.
- **Islands**: *Analyze islands* finds every part that would start printing in mid-air and paints it on the model; *Go to island* takes the camera there, from below.
- **Pre-supported files** are recognised on import. Keep them as they are, separate the supports from the model, or replace them with Breadslicer's.

### Slice

- **Review** runs with every slice: islands, parts off the plate, suction cups, hollows without drains, thin parts. *Fix all* applies every fix it can in one step, and one Ctrl+Z takes it all back.
- **Layer view** that zooms down to the printer's real pixels, with their real grey levels, as they are written in the file.
- **Time and resin** estimates. The resin count reads the lit pixels of every layer, so parts that overlap are not counted twice.
- **Export** to the formats the printers in our list read: Anycubic (ZIP and binary families), Elegoo/Chitu CTB, GOO, Creality CXDLP and more.

### Printers, resins, projects

- **157 printers**, taken from the UVtools catalog: resolution, size, mirroring and file format.
- **Resin tables** from Elegoo, Anycubic and Creality, read from the manufacturers' own published settings.
- **File › Import printer settings** reads the resins you exported from other resin slicers into your own library.
- **Projects** are saved as `.bread`, with supports and hollows still editable when you reopen them. The scene is saved in the background every two minutes, and after a crash Breadslicer offers to bring it back.

## Under the hood

Some numbers from the week before the release, all measured on our development PC (Ryzen 7 5800X, 32 GB, RTX 3090):

- **Tests:** 7,355 tests across the workspace plus 385 in the geometry engines, all passing on the tagged commit, with Clippy clean. Two hollowing tests are known to fail now and then under heavy load and pass on their own.
- **Review of a large hollow worm model**, a print of more than 2,000 layers: 58 s before the rewrite, 0.65 s after. See [The review in one sweep](/blog/review-in-one-sweep/).
- **Generating supports for 106 copies of a miniature**: 1.4 s, because copies share one computed set. Memory after opening that scene went from 2,481 MB to 260 MB. See [One raft for all](/blog/plates-arrange-one-raft/).
- **Resin estimate**: a big hollow model that used to read 388.5 ml now reads 329.944 ml. UVtools, counting the same file, says 329.953 ml.
- **Rasterising one layer** of a 11520 × 8640 panel: 0.9 ms without antialiasing, 68 ms with it.

The whole list of what changed, day by day, is in the [changelog](/changelog/).

## Before you print

- **The printer tested on real prints is the Anycubic Photon Mono M7 Pro.** Every other printer comes from the UVtools catalog, and every file format we write has been checked by reading it back with UVtools, layer by layer, but not on those machines. On any other printer, watch the first print closely: the model in the right spot on the plate, text not mirrored, exposure right.
- **The exe is not signed yet.** Windows may say "Windows protected your PC": click *More info*, then *Run anyway*.
- **Most printers have no picture** in the printer list yet (111 of 157). We only ship images we are allowed to redistribute.
- **Help › Export Diagnostics…** builds a zip that helps us understand a problem, with your folder paths and user name removed. Nothing is ever sent on its own: you choose whether to send it.

## Known issues

- **Islands after Generate.** In this build, Generate places the supports and stops; some islands may remain until you run *Analyze islands* and *Fix all*. The next build does both in the same press.
- **One raft for all** is a line raft that does not yet cut around models standing directly on the plate.
- **Windows only.** Linux is the next platform (see below).
- **No installer.** The zip is the installation; an installer comes later.

## What comes next

- **Generate does the whole job**: supports, then the islands found layer by layer, then every automatic fix, in one press and without dialogs. It is already in our test builds.
- **Linux**, packaged for Arch-based distributions first, plus a tarball and an AppImage.
- **An installer** for Windows and a signed executable.
- More printer pictures and more resin tables straight from their makers.
